First-time homebuyer

The truck is unpacked. Then a nail hole shows. Then a scuff from the sofa. Then a frame that has to go up before Sunday. This is not a garage full of tools. It is the four things that cover the first month of ordinary damage.

A box of tools, not a garage

Volume in household tool sets sits on compact cases, not 300-piece filler kits. Cartman’s 39-piece is the one that keeps landing near the top of Amazon’s Tool Sets list — hammer, tape, drivers, pliers, a case that fits a closet shelf. Amazon Basics 142-piece is the next stack if you want more bits in the same idea. Skip the mechanic 230-piece. That is a car, not a first house.

Small spackle, not a five-gallon bucket

Most first-month holes are nails and doorknob kisses. DAP Fast ’N Final in the 8 oz tub is the lightweight that sits at the top of Wall Surface Repair. 3M’s small-hole kit is the one that already includes the knife and the sanding pad, which is why it has the review pile. DryDex if you want the pink-to-white tell so you do not sand too early. One small tub. You are not floating a room.

Mini roller, brush, cup, tray

A 9-inch wall kit is how you paint a room. You are touching up a patch the size of a fist. Volume on touch-up sets sits on 4-inch mini kits that already include the frame, covers, a tray, and liners. Bates and Mister Rui keep showing up in Painting Tool Kits for that exact stack. The tray is the cup. Do not buy a separate painter’s pail unless you like washing two things.

Hang the first thing

Two piles, on purpose. Command strips own Picture Hanging Hardware — no nail, no patch later if the layout is wrong. A 300-piece hook-and-nail kit (KURUI keeps winning that search) is what you use when the frame is heavy or the wall is going to stay that way. Buy the strips first. Open the nail kit when the gallery wall is decided.

Order of use

Tools live in the closet the day you move in. Spackle after the first picture comes down or the first ding. Paint after the patch is dry, not the same afternoon. Hanging last, once you have lived in the room long enough to know where the sofa actually sits.
